UTC−11 is one of the least populated inhabited time zones on Earth and is used primarily by American Samoa, Niue, and several remote U.S. Pacific island territories. Major settlements include Pago Pago in American Samoa and Alofi in Niue. The zone lies just east of the International Date Line, making it one of the last inhabited regions to experience each calendar day. Most locations in UTC−11 do not observe Daylight Saving Time. Their tropical position near the equator means daylight length changes very little throughout the year, reducing the practical need for seasonal clock adjustments. The absence of DST also reflects the relatively small populations and localized economies of these islands.
